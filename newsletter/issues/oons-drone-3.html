Oons Drone 3

a short update

Hello all.

Welcome and welcome back.

Apologies for the extended and unexplained hiatus - my drone simulator has been crashing and failing repeatedly. Despite adjusting all the other variables, I still have the same issue with the vertical drop.

It’s even stranger because the console doesn’t show any errors or output, which makes me think there might be an issue with how the keystrokes are being processed.

I tried adjusting the mass and drag factors, which were both equally feckless!

I adjusted the liftForce by gargantuan amounts. I assumed it could be related to other variables or how I set up the simulation, so I did more investigating.

I figured out a few potential culprits, the first being that inflated liftForce and thrust values weren’t helping, so I adjusted them downward.

I also removed the Time.deltaTime variable from my code. Time.deltaTime is the amount of time that has passed since the last frame. In Unity, it's often used to ensure consistent behavior regardless of the frame rate. However, in my case, multiplying forces by Time.deltaTime inappropriately caused the forces to be applied more weakly than intended, which led to the sinking issue.

Normally, Time.deltaTime is used to scale forces over time for smoother movement. However, when misused with AddForce (which already applies forces over time), it can reduce the intended force, leading to unexpected behavior like my drone sinking faster than it should.

I also included a line to help debug and a phrase to be printed to the console.

However, my console remained blank. So, none of these fixes, though promising, helped at all. In my frustration, I then decided to test if disabling gravity and center of mass would help (on the RigidBody component); if you recall, the RigidBody component allows an object to act under the physics engine in Unity. It controls things like mass, drag, and the effect of gravity on an object. By disabling gravity and manually adjusting the center of mass, I hoped to stop the drone from dropping too quickly.

On the other hand, this still doesn’t get me any closer to my game. Disabling Use Gravity means that the drone is no longer subject to the natural force of gravity. Thus, it did not fall in this case. While the drone was previously affected too much by gravity and other forces, now it's not affected enough by external forces and remains frozen.

We have no room for indolent drones here; however, it is still very much frozen. So, while I solved one problem, we still have another.

Unfortunately, today’s newsletter wasn’t as satisfying as I had hoped, but most programming is debugging (for me, anyway).

Thanks for reading.

Until next time. Bye.
