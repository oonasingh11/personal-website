This Week's Victim: AI

AI will keep weaseling into our lives — unless you go full Luddite, you’re going to see it everywhere, personally and professionally.

I’m very pro-AI honestly, but I do keep seeing more and more alarming articles around how high schoolers and college students alike seem to be getting less sharp not just due to AI — but a confluence of other factors: less book reading, shorter attention spans, and the ever-present shortcut of AI. And of course, we’re going to address the new age question of — which AI can/will steal my job?

The 3 Types of AI

Generative AI

Generative AI creates new content, and at the risk of being obvious - it “generates” content. Generative AI is the kind most of us are familiar with—tools like ChatGPT and DALL·E that generate text, images, and other media based on prompts. These systems are trained on massive datasets and respond creatively to input. They’re great for content creation, idea generation, and prototyping.

But here’s the thing—generative AI needs to be prompted. It doesn’t initiate tasks or make decisions autonomously. It’s also susceptible to making a lot of errors. So while it can boost productivity, it’s not replacing full workflows—at least not yet!

Agentic AI

Agentic AI is probably a type of AI you are less familiar with, and the one more likely to steal your job! Agentic AI can perform tasks, and it’s kind of the direction we are seeing corporate AI agents moving towards.

Let’s say you want to plan a restaurant-focused weekend in New York. Generative AI would give you just a list of restaurants. Agentic AI:

  • Finds restaurants that match your preferences
  • Cross-checks them against your calendar
  • Books the tables
  • Schedules your Uber
  • Sends invites to your friends
  • And even texts you a reminder an hour before your reservation

Honestly, the ceiling for this tech is way higher than I can conceptualise, but this is the one I’d watch closely.

Predictive AI

Predictive AI is also incredibly exciting, and similarly to agentic AI, the average person is probably less likely to have encountered it.

Predictive AI uses massive amounts of historical data to make predictions.

It is used across various industries - healthcare, finance, insurance, and in military ops. What might be a little scarier to think about is the way predictive AI just levels up with more data. These models keep getting stronger the more they’re used. These models are like villains in sci-fi, where they keep getting stronger when you hit them and then say something insane like “hit me again.” With the hit here being more data. In chess, for example, AI models have demonstrated time and time again their ability to win, but also evolve their type of play. (If you’re curious about this, you should Google ‘centaur chess’ if unfamiliar. )

So Is ANY of this Scary? Is any of this a doomsday scenario?

(More of Robin Williams as Bicentennial Man, taken from geekvibesnation.com).

Let’s break this down now that we understand what each type of AI does.

Generative AI:

We’re probably safe on the generative AI front because it does need human prompting/creativity for it to work. It’s not autonomous, and it does need US to refine it, clean it up, etc. Generative AI boosts productivity for people, but it’s not replacing them. Still kind of dumb in a charming way. It needs us!

Agentic AI:

Eh, this could get a little weirder and freakier faster. Agentic AI can take over your tasks. They’re already getting better at admin, routine, and operational work and they will pretty soon be able to replace entire jobs completely. Moreover, all these models are getting cheaper and cheaper, and will continue to get cheaper, so the cost of ‘labor’ here will continue to decrease. So this one doesn’t need us that badly—and that’s the scary part.

Predictive AI:

This AI is pretty impressive, but the risk isn’t as high here as it is with agentic AI. It can replace statistical analysts who would have taken magnitudes of more hours to complete the same tasks/analysis. It’ll replace some junior analysts, maybe cause a few layoffs at hedge funds. Mostly, it just knows your company’s going to collapse before you do, which is still a little scary!

Why Do We Care?

AI isn’t going anywhere, and it isn’t an abstract trend. It’s technology we all need to get more familiar with, whether or not you are a dev. AI can feel a little ominous or hand-wavey—but it’s here to stay. It’ll only become more deeply embedded in both our public and private lives.

Whether AI is generating content, making predictions, or completing tasks autonomously, understanding the types of AI gives us the language to talk about what’s happening. Also, don’t you want to say agentic AI next time you’re at a bar and kind of know something about it?
